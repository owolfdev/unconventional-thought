## Description

```
Did Bon Scott know he was going to die?

Not the date. Not the hour. But listen to Highway to Hell — really listen — and something shifts. Bon's voice on the title track isn't the coiled-spring aggression of early AC/DC. It's loose. Warm. Like a man who's made a decision and is completely okay with it.

He recorded "I'm on the highway to hell" eight months before he was found dead in a car in London. Was that swagger — or acceptance?

In this video we follow one continuous argument:

• Why Bon's vocal change on Highway to Hell sounds like something other than alcohol
• The Robert Johnson crossroads story — and the "devil's bargain" artists have made for centuries
• The 27 Club, the cost of greatness, and why Bon at 33 still fits the pattern
• Heroin, the London scene, and why a different cause of death would have been commercially and culturally catastrophic in 1980
• Disco Demolition, radio gatekeepers, and the story America was ready to accept
• Why the full toxicology from Bon Scott's inquest was never made public — 45 years later
• Back in Black five months later: fifty million copies and a machine that needed the simplest explanation

This isn't medical verdict. It's rock history, cultural context, and a close listen to the art Bon was making at the end.

Play Highway to Hell tonight. Loud. Then tell me he didn't know.

—
The Unconventional Thought
Documentary-style deep dives into the stories nobody's telling.
```
